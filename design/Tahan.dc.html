From your answers

The feed is the product

Everything — a meal photo, a pill taken, a recipe, a birthday — lands in one warm private feed. Routines and recipes are things that live in the village, and posting is how they get seen.

Four tabs: Home (feed + today), Village (the shelf: cookbook, lists, albums, routines, people), +, Me.

Flow

Invite-first, never public

Create village → name + what it's for → invite by link or contact → first post is prompted ("say hello"). Joining is only ever via an invite; a person can sit in many villages and switches from the pill in the header. No discovery, no follower counts, no strangers.

Your four notes, handled

Mom's glance → the Today strip sits above the feed; a given dose reads "Given by Dad · 8:02am" without opening anything.

Son's quick meal → "Ate" is its own one-tap post: photo, no caption needed.

Albums → photos posted in the same few days gather into a named album automatically.

Cookbook → recipes are stored as handed-down cards, with who it came from and a note in their voice.

Decisions

No categories, no reports, no queue. Twelve people who know each other don't need a taxonomy of violations — they need one person to say why. A dropdown would let an admin remove something without ever facing the author.

The reason is optional but discouraged in copy, because a silent removal is the thing that actually breaks a family. The blank state says so out loud.

It's a deletion, not a shadow-hide. Tahan never keeps a copy of something it told you it removed — that's the same promise as leaving a village, and it has to hold here or it holds nowhere.

A record, visible to admins only: who removed what, when, and the reason they gave. Enough that a second admin can ask about it; not a public log that turns into a scoreboard.

15cThe printed recipe card, at real size. A 6 × 4 inch card, both sides — the method on the front, Rosa's story and the village's notes on the back. It prints at true size on any paper and comes out of the printer as the thing you'd actually put in a recipe box. Opens as Tahan Recipe Card.
Print

Body type is 11pt and the ingredient column is fixed, so it stays readable in a kitchen at arm's length — the reason the card is 6 × 4 rather than the more common 5 × 3.

The back carries what a printed recipe usually loses: who it belongs to, when it entered the cookbook, and the three notes the family left on it. "Kept by Abuela Rosa" is on the card because the recipe is hers even once it's on your counter.

13dThe rules behind the three screens, and where an event shows up.
Decisions

An event is a post, not a calendar. It appears in the feed as a card with the date, the house and the count coming, and it stays pinned to the top of the feed until the day after. There is no month grid anywhere in Tahan.

Only the host can change the facts — when, where, the title. Anyone can add to either board, and anyone can take back a row they claimed; a claimed row shows a face, so a change of plan is visible without an announcement.

Notifications stay rare, in keeping with 9a: one when the event is created, one the evening before with your own row on it ("you're bringing the ponche"), and nothing else. Claims and votes never notify.

Notes work as everywhere else — the event has one thread, so the "can we come at seven?" conversation doesn't get split across two boards.

12The Flutter handoff package
12aOne document a developer can build from. The nine palettes as exact values with a ThemeData sketch, both painters specified (authoring boxes, the five path commands, the absolute-coordinate rule, drift on one controller), the screen inventory with the rules the UI has to enforce, and the four things still open. Opens as Tahan Flutter Handoff — printable as-is.
Handoff

Written as a contract rather than a description: where the doc and the prototype disagree, the prototype wins. That keeps 1a the single source of truth for spacing, copy and feel.

The two constraints worth arguing about are in there in plain language — path data stays absolute (the scaler offsets every number as an x,y pair), and the drift draws zero particles when animations are off rather than slowing down.

Not designed yet, and flagged as such: sync and offline. The feed has to open in a farmhouse kitchen with one bar, so the local store should be decided before the first screen is coded.

7Glass, sill and parallax — the last of the window
7aThe scene drifts as you scroll. The backdrop moves at about a sixth of the feed's speed, so the sky sits behind the room rather than travelling with it. Scroll the feed in 1a and watch the ridge line hold still while the cards pass.
Detail

The layer is scaled 6% so it can travel without ever exposing an edge, and it's moved on the compositor — no re-render per scroll frame, which is what keeps it smooth on an older phone.

In Flutter this is the same idea: a Stack with the painted scene offset by the scroll controller, not a re-layout.

7bGlass and a sill. A faint diagonal sheen across the pane, a soft vignette at the edges, and a warm ledge where the glass meets the room — the three cues that make the veil read as glass rather than as a scrim. Turn off with Window framing in Tweaks.
framed

Kept deliberately quiet: no beading, no bevels, no reflections that could be mistaken for content. The window should only be noticed on the second look.

5eNotes for a Flutter build. What matters for keeping this look on both platforms.
Handoff

The scenes and avatars are flat layered shapes with no photographic assets — they belong in a CustomPainter (or a small SVG set), sized to the header box and cropped to a circle for avatars. The nine palettes become nine ThemeData objects; picking a scene swaps the theme, exactly as it does here.

The drift is a handful of shapes on one repeating AnimationController — cheap, and it must respect MediaQuery.disableAnimations. The time-of-day wash is a single gradient overlay driven by the clock.

Two things to keep platform-native rather than styled: sheets, and the invite share action. Everything else is drawn by the app, so it looks identical on both.
